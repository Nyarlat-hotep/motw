Monster of the Week

Hunter's Journal

What is Monster of the Week?

You play a monster hunter. Each mystery is its own episode: a creepy lead, an investigation, a monster to understand and stop. One person, the Keeper, runs the world and the monsters. Everyone else plays a hunter.

D&DMonster of the Week
Diced20 plus other diceTwo six-sided dice plus one stat
CombatInitiative, turns, AC, hit pointsNo initiative. A fight is one more thing in the story
When you rollAttacks, checks, savesOnly when you trigger a move
DamageRolledA fixed harm number from the weapon or attack
FailingUsually nothing happensThe Keeper makes a move, and you earn XP

The goal is not a fair fight. It is to investigate, learn the monster's weakness, and use it before the monster, or the clock, gets to people.

The roll: 2d6 plus a stat
TotalResultMeaning
10+Full successYou do it, often with a bonus
7-9Partial successYou do it, with a catch: a cost, complication or hard choice
6 or lessMissThe Keeper makes a move against you. Mark an experience point
  • +Stat: add the stat the move names. Stats usually run from -1 to +3.
  • +1 forward: a bonus to your very next roll only.
  • +1 ongoing: a bonus to every roll that fits, until the situation changes.
  • Hold: tokens you spend later, one at a time, usually to ask the Keeper a question.
  • 12+ (Advanced): extra results marked Advanced on your reference sheet. They apply once your hunter has taken the matching advancement. Confirm with your Keeper.

When do I roll? Only when what your hunter does matches a move's trigger, like "when you get into a fight and kick some ass." Otherwise the Keeper just tells you what happens.

Hunter Agenda

  • Act like you are the hero of this story (because you are).
  • Make your own destiny.
  • Find the damn monsters and stop them.
  • Play your hunter like a real person.
The basic moves

Every hunter can use these. Press Roll it to jump to the roller with the move picked.

Magic

When you use magic, say what you are trying to achieve and how you do the spell, then roll +Weird. On a 10+ it works without issues and you choose an effect. On a 7-9 you choose an effect and a glitch, and the Keeper decides what the glitch does.

Effects

    Glitches

      The Keeper may also say that...

      The spell needs weird materials, takes 10 seconds, 30 seconds or a minute, needs ritual chanting and gestures, needs arcane symbols, needs one or two helpers, or needs a tome.

      Big Magic

      For more than Use Magic can do. Tell the Keeper what you want and they list the requirements: days or weeks of research, experiments with failures, rare ingredients, hours or days of casting, a crowd of helpers (2, 3, 7, 13 or more), a particular place or time, magic as part of the ritual, or a specific danger. Meet them and it works. The preparation is the adventure.

      No Use Magic on your playbook? Treat spells as Big Magic, and use your playbook's Weird move.

      Harm, healing and Luck
      HarmHow seriousHealing
      0Minor, short-term effectsHealed right away
      1-3An ordinary injuryFirst aid, then rest. Heal 1 harm when you do
      4-7Serious and unstable. Mark Unstable. It gets worse unless treatedFirst aid to stabilize. Full healing needs a healing move, infirmary time or magic
      8+Kills a normal human, including a hunterNone

      Armor reduces harm by its rating. At the end of a mystery, heal 1 harm if there was no chance to rest, or all harm if there was plenty of time.

      Luck

      You spend Luck, you never roll it. One point lets you reduce a wound you just suffered to 0 harm, or change a roll's result to a 12. With no Luck left, bad things will happen to you.

      Experience and leveling up

      Mark XP when a roll totals 6 or less, or when a move tells you to. When you mark the fifth box, level up: erase the marks and choose an improvement. After five level-ups you can pick advanced improvements too.

      End of session

      • Did we conclude the current mystery?
      • Did we save someone from certain death (or worse)?
      • Did we learn something new and important about the world?
      • Did we learn something new and important about one of the hunters?

      One or two yes answers: everyone marks 1 XP. Three or four: everyone marks 2.

      Weird moves for hunters without Use Magic

      All roll +Weird. Your playbook gives you one or two of these.

      Behind the Keeper's screen

      Knowing what your Keeper is doing makes the game less mysterious. Their agenda: make the world seem real, play to see what happens, and make the hunters' lives dangerous and scary.

      • Nothing is safe, everything is a threat. Expect people, places and things to turn on you.
      • They talk to your hunter, not to you. "Your hunter hears a scratching at the door."
      • They ask questions and build on your answers. What you say becomes part of the world.
      • Half-wins are normal. Sometimes you get exactly what you earned, not everything you wanted.

      How a mystery is built

      Basic conceptThe core idea: an urban legend, a loose end from a past case, a good place for a monster to lurk
      HookThe clue that catches the hunters' attention
      ThreatsMonsters, minions, bystanders and locations
      CountdownWhat happens if the hunters never show up, in six steps: Day, Shadows, Sunset, Dusk, Nightfall, Midnight

      Monsters have powers and a weakness, usually attack for 3 to 5 harm, wear 1 to 2 armor and take 8 to 12 harm to kill. Monsters may not be defeated until you use their weakness, which is why Investigate a Mystery matters so much.

      When you miss

      The Keeper makes a move: separate you, inflict harm, reveal future badness, take your stuff, put someone in trouble, offer an opportunity at a cost, or tell you the consequences and ask if you still want to go ahead. Then they ask what you do.

      Phenomena

      Sometimes the threat is a phenomenon, not a creature: Alien, Artifact, Biohazard, Bubble, Conspiracy, Corruption, Experiment, Mutagen, Panic, Portal, Teratogen or Zone. When you investigate one you can ask: How is this phenomenon doing this? What could fix, cure or slow it down? How far does the effect reach? What will be affected next?

      Running the game (for the Keeper)

      For whoever runs the game. It is a plain-English summary of the Keeper's reference sheets; the printed sheets have the final word. When it is time to wrap up, the End the session button on Play walks everyone through XP and healing.

      Your job

      Three goals: make the world feel real, play to find out what happens, and make the hunters' lives dangerous and scary. When you say what happens next, let four things guide you: the principles below, the rules, what you prepared, and honesty.

      Principles

      • Find the horror in everyday places
      • Talk to the hunters, not the players
      • Use your moves without naming them
      • Root for the hunters
      • Build the mythology as you play, and keep it consistent
      • Nothing is safe
      • Name everyone they meet and make them feel like ordinary people
      • Ask questions and build on the answers
      • Sometimes give them exactly what they earned, not all they wanted
      • Ask what is happening off-screen
      • Do not always decide what happens
      • Everything is a threat

      Your moves

      When the hunters miss, or look to you to see what happens next, pick one. After every move, ask what they do.

      • Split the group up
      • Show badness coming
      • Show badness happening out of sight
      • Do the harm you already set up
      • Make them investigate
      • Make them get something
      • Warn of the consequences and ask if they still want to go ahead
      • Turn their own move back on them
      • Offer a chance, maybe at a price
      • Take some of their stuff away
      • Put someone in trouble
      • Make a threat move from your mystery or your arc
      • After every move, ask what they do next

      When a hunter is hurt

      Every time anyone gets hurt, use one of these.

      0-harm or moreThrown off for a moment, drop something, or take -1 forward
      1-harm or moreFall down, take -1 ongoing, pass out, or intense pain
      Unstable woundsThey can get worse: add 1 harm
      8-harm or moreDying or dead

      Threat moves

      Each kind of threat has its own list. Use them when a threat acts on its own.

      Monsters

      • Hint that it is near
      • Show up suddenly
      • Show its full might
      • Attack with great force and fury
      • Seize someone or something
      • Attack with stealth and planning
      • Order its underlings to do awful things
      • Destroy something
      • Escape, however well it was held
      • Give chase
      • Retreat to its home ground
      • Brag and taunt, maybe letting a secret slip
      • Come back from apparent destruction
      • Use an unnatural power

      Minions

      • Burst into sudden, wild violence
      • Attack together in a planned way
      • Grab someone or steal something
      • Give away a secret
      • Bring someone or something to the master
      • Give chase
      • Make a threat or demand for the master
      • Run away
      • Use an unnatural power
      • Show a flicker of conscience or humanity
      • Defy their master in some small way

      Bystanders

      • Wander off alone
      • Argue with the hunters
      • Get in the way
      • Reveal something
      • Admit their fears
      • Panic in terror
      • Try to help the hunters
      • Try to protect people
      • Show they are not up to it
      • Look for help or comfort

      Locations

      • Present a hazard
      • Reveal something
      • Hide something
      • Close a way
      • Open a way
      • Reshape itself
      • Trap someone
      • Offer a guide
      • Present a guardian
      • Stop working the way it should
      • Set a particular mood

      Making a mystery

      Where an idea can come from: an urban legend or folklore monster, a loose end from an earlier mystery, a problem from one of your longer story arcs, a plot borrowed from somewhere else with the details changed, a place you know that would suit a monster, something the hunters want to check out, or the return of a monster, bystander or location from before. Then write the hook (the clue that catches the hunters' attention), create the threats, and set the countdown: what happens if the hunters never come, in six steps (Day, Shadows, Sunset, Dusk, Nightfall, Midnight).

      At the end of a session

      Ask the four questions: did they conclude the mystery, did they save someone from certain death (or worse), did you learn something new and important about the world, and did you learn something new and important about one of the hunters. One or two yes answers is 1 XP for each hunter, three or four is 2. When a mystery ends, a hunter who had no chance to rest heals 1 harm, and one who had plenty of time heals all of it.
